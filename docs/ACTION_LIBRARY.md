# Culinary action directing library

Use only actions required by the recipe. Describe real contact and visible results. Adapt tools to the verified period or explicitly use a modern reconstruction kitchen.

| Operation | Opening state and hand setup | Readable motion | Endpoint and continuity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crack one egg | One intact egg in right hand; empty bowl below; left hand free | Tap once on flat surface; both thumbs open the fracture above bowl; contents fall by gravity | One yolk and white in bowl; two shell halves retained; remove shells before next operation |
| Separate an egg | Intact egg, receiving bowl and separate yolk bowl | Use a chosen demonstrated separation method; split opening from separation if too complex | Yolk goes to its own vessel; white to its own vessel; do not show liquid cloning |
| Whip whites | Clean dry bowl; whites only; left hand steadies rim; right holds whisk | Repeated whisk strokes through liquid/foam, plausible resistance; show a short process excerpt | Establish later states in separate clips after real elapsed time; no instant stiff peaks |
| Test stiff peaks | Already-whipped glossy foam; whisk in foam | Lift whisk slowly; foam strand separates and leaves upright peak | Peak remains standing; no watery reset or unexplained extra foam |
| Add flour | Measured flour in small vessel; receiving bowl reachable | Controlled tilt produces falling stream; source visibly empties | Flour remains visible on mixture; source empty or accurately partly used |
| Fold flour | Foam with flour on top; spatula at near rim | Cut down, sweep under, lift and turn; rotate bowl slightly with free hand | One/few folds incorporated; remaining flour reduces gradually; do not instantly dissolve all flour |
| Slice meat | Stable board; one piece of meat; left claw grip well clear; right grips knife | One controlled down-and-forward stroke, blade contacts board | One slice separates and rests beside remaining piece; no extra slices appear |
| Crush herbs | Herbs in mortar; left supports mortar; right grips pestle | Short presses and grinding rotations against inner wall | Bruised/crushed herbs stay inside; texture changes locally |
| Pour liquid | Source vessel tilted over visible receiving vessel | Continuous gravity stream with plausible splash/viscosity | Source decreases and receiver increases; no endless stream |
| Knead dough | Cohesive dough on board; palms placed on surface | Push with heel, fold back, quarter-turn | Dough stretches/compresses; no hand penetration or instant texture change |
| Roll dough | Dough disk; rolling pin across it; hands at ends | Press and roll away then return | Dough becomes slightly wider/thinner; pin retains shape; thickness changes gradually |
| Laminate pastry | Chilled dough enclosing butter; floured board | Roll a small amount or show one fold; split stages into separate clips | Butter stays enclosed; ledger tracks turns and rest time; no instant finished layers |
| Shape crescent | One cut dough triangle, tip pointing away | Roll broad base toward tip using fingertips; gently curve ends | One unbaked crescent; no baked crust yet |
| Sear meat | Hot suitable pan, small oil amount, raw meat on utensil | Lower meat away from cook; release; surface contact begins | Meat rests in pan; initial sizzle; browned endpoint requires later elapsed time |
| Stir/simmer | Liquid already at appropriate heating stage | Spoon displaces contents in slow arcs; bubbles consistent with heat | Ingredient pieces remain count/size-consistent; simmer is not violent boil |
| Bake | Shaped raw food and suitable oven setup | Load with appropriate tool; keep hand distance safe | Raw food enters oven; later baked result is a distinct time-jump shot |
| Serve | Finished food; empty plate/bowl; serving utensil | Lift one defined portion and lower onto plate | Source portion decreases; plate receives that portion; no duplicates |

## Failure correction

If a generated hand fuses with a tool, reduce the action complexity, make contact visible and regenerate the affected clip from a corrected keyframe. For egg-shell errors, separate tapping from opening. For knife errors, use one slice and a locked side view. For whipping errors, show one already-established texture stage per clip. For pouring errors, use a shorter pour and a clearly visible source rim/destination. Do not try to fix every failure by adding more camera movement.

## Pastry history boundary

A kipferl, a modern laminated croissant and another crescent bread must not be treated as one recipe without evidence. Establish which dish/version is being reconstructed. A modern croissant demonstration should be clearly labeled modern if the episode investigates earlier origin stories. The same rule applies to modern coffee equipment and named historical coffee drinks.
